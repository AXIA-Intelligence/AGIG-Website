Frequently asked questions

What happens when a better AI model comes along?

There will always be a newer, better model. What matters is what you own underneath it. Your governance, your training, and the lessons your system has already learned live in a layer that belongs to you, not to the model. When something better arrives, it's available to you right away, and there's nothing to rebuild.

That is not a promise for the future. It is how the current system runs. When Moonshot released Kimi K2, we were using it in a governed way the same day. No migration. No rebuild.

Why not hire an assistant?

Hire the assistant. This does not replace one, and the two are stronger together. A good assistant brings judgment, relationships, and hands in the real world that no system provides. What a person cannot do is keep the full record of everything you are responsible for, across every domain, without gaps, year after year. People manage a great deal, and then they forget, get overloaded, or move on. The system is the continuity underneath all of it.

It also makes an assistant more effective, because you use it to shape and track what you hand off, so requests are clear, reasoning travels with them, and nothing quietly drops. When you are ready, the assistant can work inside the same system with access you scope, so what they produce flows back into your record.

How much work will this require from me?

Less than you would expect, and most of it is at the start. Onboarding is done in under a day. There is a short learning curve, shaped by how much you have already worked with tools like this. You talk to the system in plain language, the way you would talk to a capable person. There is nothing to memorize.

During the onboarding phase you have a direct line to the founder and a regular check-in cadence, so every question has a place to land. Your ongoing part is only what needs you. Setting priorities, approving what the system can reach, correcting it when it is wrong, and signing off on anything consequential.

What information does the system access?

Only sources you approve and that are appropriate to the purpose. The system may connect broadly across your operating world, but permissions remain explicit and minimum-necessary at every level. Account, folder, database, workflow, action. You set the boundaries.

Some domains may remain intentionally separated where privacy, professional authority, ethics, security, or personal preference requires it. That is a governance decision, not a limitation.

Will it become another system to manage?

No. It runs inside the tools you already use. Your interface stays the same, whether that is Claude, Gemini, or ChatGPT. The governance, the memory, and the routing happen underneath. You do not administer a new platform. You direct a system that keeps its own state.

Why does it cost this much?

You are not buying software. You are buying a finished, working system built around your business, plus the time to install it and teach you to run it. The system persists your memory, your rules, and your standards, and gets better the more you use it.

Pricing scales with the complexity of your world, not by a fixed rate card. You pay once for the deployment, and the value keeps compounding from there.
